Grade Identification

8.8 — Standard High-Tensile
Most common industrial grade. 800 MPa tensile. Marked "8.8" on head.
10.9 — High Strength
Heavy machinery, dynamic loading. 1,040 MPa tensile. Marked "10.9" on head.
12.9 — Ultra High Strength
Socket cap screws. 1,220 MPa tensile. Use only where specified. Marked "12.9" or on socket head.
A2-70 / A4-80 — Stainless
A2 = 304 SS · A4 = 316 marine. Always use copper anti-seize to prevent galling.

Torque Adjustment Factors — Thread Condition

Thread Condition K-Factor Multiply Table Value By Notes
Dry, uncoated steel (baseline) 0.20 × 1.00 Use main table values as-is
Electrozinc / bright zinc plated 0.20 × 1.00 Similar friction to bare steel
Hot-dip galvanised (HDG) 0.25 × 1.25 ↑ Rough zinc coating — increase torque 25% to achieve same preload. Common mistake is to under-torque HDG bolts.
Lightly oiled (SAE 30 / machine oil) 0.15 × 0.75 ↓ Reduce torque 25%. Applying dry values to oiled bolts will over-tension.
Copper anti-seize compound 0.13 × 0.65 ↓ Reduce torque 35%. Standard for stainless and high-temp assemblies. Already applied in SS table above.
Molybdenum disulfide (MoS₂) 0.13 × 0.65 ↓ Reduce torque 35%. Common in high-load / close-tolerance assemblies.
Loctite threadlocker (before cure) 0.15 × 0.75 ↓ Loctite acts as lubricant before curing. Follow product datasheet for specific product.
Example: M12 Grade 8.8, hot-dip galvanised → 71 × 1.25 = 89 Nm
Example: M16 Grade 10.9, oiled threads → 260 × 0.75 = 195 Nm

Torque Wrench Selection Guide

Bolt Range Drive Size Recommended Wrench Range Notes
M3–M6 1/4" drive or torque screwdriver 1–25 Nm Use a torque screwdriver — a ratchet wrench is too imprecise at this scale
M6–M12 3/8" drive 5–60 Nm Stay within 20–80% of wrench maximum for best accuracy
M10–M16 1/2" drive 20–200 Nm Most common range on site
M16–M24 3/4" drive 100–800 Nm Do not use a 1/2" drive wrench above M16 Grade 10.9 — exceeds drive capacity
  • Click-type wrenches: Stop immediately on click — continuing to apply force after the click over-torques the bolt.
  • Multi-bolt joints (flanges, covers): Tighten in a cross/star pattern in three passes — 30%, 70%, 100% of final torque.
  • Calibration: Click-type wrenches should be calibrated annually or every 5,000 cycles. Store wound back to lowest setting.
  • Fine pitch threads (MF): Add approximately 10% to coarse-pitch table values to achieve the same preload.
Drill & Tap Sizes — Metric Threads
  • Tap drill formula: Tap drill ≈ Major diameter − Pitch (coarse). E.g. M10 coarse (pitch 1.5mm) → 10 − 1.5 = 8.5mm.
  • Fine pitch: Use the fine column where a finer thread is specified — always confirm pitch on drawing.
  • Clearance holes: Close fit shown. For free fit (easier assembly), go one size up. Use free fit for through-bolted joints where alignment is loose.
  • Drill sizing: Use the nearest standard drill size if the exact size is unavailable — never go smaller than the tap drill diameter.
