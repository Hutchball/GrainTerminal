## 1. Liverpool Dock Grain Terminal History

### Royal Seaforth Dock
- **Opened:** 1971
- **Grain terminal completed:** End of 1972 (partial use from July 1972)
- **Silo capacity:** 100,000 tonnes
- **Location:** Part of Royal Seaforth Dock complex, 500 acres reclaimed from the Mersey
- **Source:** [Hansard Parliamentary Record, 25 July 1974](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1974/jul/25/seaforth-grain-terminal)

### Alexandra Dock (Earlier Facility)
- **Built:** 1874-1882 by George Fosbery Lyster
- **Opened:** 1881
- **Historical grain silo capacity:** 110,000 tons (with 20,000 tons floor space)
- **Current use:** Primarily scrap metal export (grain operations moved to Seaforth)
- **Source:** [Wikipedia - Alexandra Dock, Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Dock,_Liverpool)

### Current Operator
- **Peel Ports** operates Liverpool port facilities including grain terminal
- **Status:** UK's leading gateway for imports of grain and animal feed
- **Source:** [Peel Ports Liverpool](https://www.peelports.com/port-locations/liverpool/)

## 2. UK Regulatory Framework for Grain Terminals

### Primary Legislation

| Regulation | Description | Source |
|------------|-------------|--------|
| **DSEAR 2002** | Dangerous Substances and Explosive Atmospheres Regulations | [HSE ATEX Page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/fireandexplosion/atex.htm) |
| **ATEX Directives** | Equipment and protective systems for explosive atmospheres | [Wikipedia ATEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATEX_directives) |

### Zone Classifications for Dust

| Zone | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Zone 20** | Explosive dust cloud present for long periods |
| **Zone 21** | Explosive dust cloud occurs regularly during normal operations |
| **Zone 22** | Explosive dust cloud likely only rarely but still possible |

**Equipment Requirements:**
- Zone 22: ATEX Category 3D equipment
- Zone 21: ATEX Category 2D equipment
- Zone 20: ATEX Category 1D equipment

**Source:** [HSE ATEX Guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/fireandexplosion/atex.htm)

### Key HSE Publication

**HSG103: Safe Handling of Combustible Dusts - Precautions Against Explosions**
- Second edition, 2003
- Covers grain, flour, and other organic dusts
- Free PDF available from HSE
- **Direct link:** [HSE HSG103 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg103.pdf)
- **Source:** [HSE Publications](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/hsg103.htm)

### Grain-Specific HSE Requirements (from HSG103 and HSE guidance)

1. **Building Design:** Locate plant in open air or strong steel-framed building with lightweight panel walls (for explosion relief)

2. **Silos/Bins:** Must be fitted with explosion relief venting to unoccupied place of safety (preferably outside)

3. **Bucket Elevators:** Must be equipped with explosion relief at head of elevator and as close as practicable to boot (exception: wooden elevators)

4. **Dust Collectors:** Open bag filter units must be totally enclosed and fitted with explosion relief

5. **Rotary Valves:** Only type-tested, ATEX-certified rotary valves should be used as explosion chokes

6. **Review Cycle:** HSE recommends assessment review every 3-5 years minimum, with immediate reassessment after any process change

**Source:** [HSE Food Industry Dust Explosions](https://www.hse.gov.uk/food/safety-hazards/dustexplosion.htm)

## 3. Typical Grain Terminal Equipment

### Bucket Elevators (Grain Legs)
- Vertically-oriented conveyor belt with scoops (buckets)
- Used to elevate grain to silos and processing
- Industry capacities: 1,350 BPH to 32,000+ BPH
- Heights up to 80m
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia - Grain Elevator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_elevator), [SKE Industries](https://www.skecon.com/product/bucket-elevator/)

### Ship Unloader (Site-Specific)
- **CORRECTION:** Original marine leg unloaders are defunct and removed from site
- **Current equipment:** Siwertell BMH (Bulk Material Handling) screw-type ship unloader
- **Manufacturer:** Bruks Siwertell
- **Technology:** Enclosed screw conveyor system - low dust emissions, minimal grain degradation
- **Typical capacities:** 200-1,800+ TPH depending on model
- **Source:** [Bruks Siwertell](https://bruks-siwertell.com/ship-unloading)

### Conveyors
- **Drag Chain Conveyors:** Runs up to 200 feet, capacities up to 30,000 BPH
- **Incline Drag Conveyors:** Inclines up to 60 degrees, capacities up to 25,000 BPH
- **Source:** [Sudenga Industries](https://sudenga.com/our-products/category/grain-handling-equipment/bucket-elevators/)
